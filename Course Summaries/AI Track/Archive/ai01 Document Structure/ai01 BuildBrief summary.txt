Course: Web Design AI track (archived version)
Lesson: ai01 Document Structure
Type of document: build brief (project task)

In one sentence:
This brief tells you to direct Claude to build a complete, valid About Me page, using a required list of technical terms, and then have Claude explain every line of it.

Main ideas:
The brief pairs with html01 Document Structure. It is your first real build, the same About Me page the main course starts with, but built by direction instead of typing.

1. What the page must contain:
- The full HTML5 skeleton: doctype, html, head, and body.
- In the head: a title (your name plus something), the charset declaration, and the viewport tag.
- One heading level 1, which is your name.
- At least two heading level 2s for sections, such as interests, goals, or favorite anything.
- Real paragraphs under each section. You dictate actual content about yourself; a page of placeholder text is a failed build.
- An HTML comment near the top with the date and the words "built by direction."
2. The must-say list. Your directions must use these terms by name; the brief calls this week one of the vocabulary being the curriculum:
- doctype, and ask Claude what happens without it. The answer goes in your prompt log.
- head versus body. Direct content into the right one and say why, for example: put the title in the head, the heading goes in the body, because they are different things.
- title, and how it differs from a heading level 1.
- charset and the viewport tag. Describe-level is fine: say what each is for as you ask for it.
- heading levels: one h1, h2s below it, no skipping.
- paragraph elements, not line breaks pretending to be paragraphs.
- comment.
3. The twist. After the build, ask Claude to read the page one line at a time and, after each line, give one short sentence on what that line does. Save this annotated walk-through in your prompt log. The goal is that you can explain every line of an HTML document you never typed, which the brief says is the point of the whole track.

Key terms:
doctype — the declaration at the very top that tells the browser this is an HTML5 document.
html — the root element that wraps the whole page.
head — the part of the document holding information about the page, such as the title, charset, and viewport.
body — the part of the document holding visible content.
title — the page name shown in the browser tab and spoken when the page opens; different from the h1.
charset — the character encoding declaration (such as UTF-8).
viewport tag — a meta tag that controls how the page scales on mobile screens.
h1 and h2 — the main heading and section headings.
paragraph element (p) — a real paragraph, as opposed to text split with line breaks.
comment — a note in the code that visitors never see or hear.

What you are asked to do:
- Direct Claude to build the About Me page with every item listed above.
- Use every must-say term by name in your directions.
- Ask Claude what happens without a doctype and record the answer in the prompt log.
- Get the line-by-line annotated walk-through and save it in the prompt log.
- Commit: aboutme.html; ai01_PromptLog.md (including the line-by-line walk-through); and your completed ai01_AudioLab.md and ai01_ConceptCheck.md.

Watch out for:
- Placeholder text means the build fails.
- Only one h1, and no skipped heading levels.
- Do not accept line breaks in place of paragraph elements.
- The title and the h1 are different things in different parts of the document.
