Summary of: ai01_AudioLab.md
Course: Web Design AI track (archived version)
Lesson: ai01 Document Structure
Type of document: audio lab (screen reader audit of your own build)

In one sentence:
This lab has you audit your own aboutme.html page by ear, checking the title, the headings outline, and the content, then deliberately break the page twice to hear what a missing title and a skipped heading level do.

Main ideas:
In ai00 you practiced on a page that was already correct. Now you audit a page you directed, so the correct answers come from your own build brief. Before each step, remind yourself what you asked Claude for, because that is what you are listening for. Setup: open aboutme.html in the browser, asking Claude for steps if needed.

1. Step 1, the page announcement. The first thing spoken when a page opens is the title you directed Claude to put in the document head. You should hear your exact title, for example "About Jordan Smith." If you hear a filename ("aboutme dot html") or something generic like "Untitled Document," the title is missing or wrong. Tell Claude what title was announced and what you asked for, and ask it to fix the title element and save. Reload and listen again.
2. Step 2, the headings list. The brief called for exactly one heading level 1 (your name) and at least two heading level 2s for sections. Run the list-all-headings command and note each heading's text and level. You should hear your name at level 1, then each section title at level 2 in the order you asked. Compare it to the brief like comparing a food order to the receipt. If something is off, such as a missing section, a wrong level, or two level 1s, describe it precisely. The example fix request: the headings list shows two heading level 1s but a page should have exactly one, so make my name the only h1, change the other to an h2, and save. Precise fix requests are a graded skill.
3. Step 3, read the whole page. This checks two things: the paragraphs contain your own dictated content, not filler, and the comment you asked for is never spoken, because comments are for developers and visitors never encounter them. From the top, read continuously to the end. You should hear your name and each section with your actual sentences as separate paragraphs, and you should not hear the comment words ("built by direction"). If you hear filler such as "lorem ipsum," tell Claude to replace the placeholder text with your dictated content and read your sentences back first to confirm. If the comment is read aloud, the comment markup is wrong; tell Claude exactly that.
4. Step 4, break it twice on purpose. You order two mistakes, hear the damage, and have them undone.
- Experiment A, the missing title: ask Claude to remove the title element and save. Reload and listen; you should now hear the filename or something generic, which is what every visitor would get before any content. Write what was announced and one sentence on what a missing title costs. Then have Claude put the title element back exactly as it was and save.
- Experiment B, the skipped heading level: ask Claude to change your first heading level 2 into a heading level 4 and save. Run the headings list and listen for the gap, level 1 jumping straight to level 4. Write how the list sounded and why the page's outline became harder to trust. Then have Claude restore it to level 2 and save.
- Confirm the repairs by running the headings list again and checking it matches your brief.

Key terms:
Title element — the element in the head that sets the page title spoken on load.
Heading level 1 (h1) — the page's single main heading, here your name.
Heading level 2 (h2) — section headings below the h1.
Skipped heading level — jumping from one level to a deeper one with none between, such as h1 to h4.
Placeholder text (lorem ipsum) — filler text that is not real content.
HTML comment — a developer note in the code that is never spoken.

What you are asked to do:
- Complete Steps 1 to 3 and write what you heard under each, fixing any failures with Claude.
- Complete both experiments in Step 4, write your findings from both, and confirm the repairs.
- Verdict: write the two-or-three-sentence verdict at the end of the lab (its prompts are in the original file); it is part of the completed AudioLab you turn in.

Watch out for:
- Two h1 headings, missing sections, or skipped levels.
- Filler text instead of your real content.
- A comment that gets read aloud means broken comment markup.
- Be sure to restore the title and the heading level after the experiments.
