Summary of: ai02_AudioLab.md
Course: Web Design AI track (archived version)
Lesson: ai02 Semantic HTML
Type of document: audio lab (screen reader audit of your own build)

In one sentence:
This lab audits your ai02 page's semantic structure, headings outline, and two lists by ear, then has you build a broken copy with plain divs to hear what semantic elements were doing for you.

Main ideas:
The audit checks the page Claude built for the ai02 brief: the semantic structure (header, nav, main, sections, footer), the headings outline, and your two lists. Keep the brief nearby as your answer key. For every step, if the page fails, describe the failure to Claude in specific words, have it fix the markup, save, and re-run the step. Record every fix in your findings; the lab calls the fix cycle the best part, not a detour. Setup: open the page in your browser.

1. Step 1, the headings list. The brief asked for one heading level 1 (the site title) and a level 2 for each of your two sections. A stranger should be able to tell what the page is and what is in it from this list alone. You should hear the site title as the only level 1, then each section title at level 2, in page order, with no skipped levels. If it fails, say exactly what you heard and what it should be, and ask Claude to fix the heading elements and save.
2. Step 2, the region walk. The brief required a real header, nav, main, and footer. Only the true semantic elements announce as regions; content wrapped in a plain generic container (a div) is invisible to this walk. From the top, press the next region command repeatedly. You should hear, in order: "banner," "navigation," "main," "content information" (how footers usually announce). If a zone is missing, for example navigation never announces, the content is probably built as a div instead of a nav element, which is exactly the defect this unit exists to catch. Example fix request: the region walk goes straight from banner to main and the navigation is not announcing as a region; check whether the nav uses a real nav element, fix it, and save.
3. Step 3, the lists read in context. The brief called for one ordered (numbered) list and one unordered (bulleted) list, each chosen to fit its content. Real lists announce themselves (for example "list of four items") and count along; the ordered list announces numbers. Read continuously through both sections. You should hear each list announced with its item count, and numbered items in the ordered list (the lab's example is "one: preheat the oven"). Check that the numbered content really needs its order: if the numbering sounds arbitrary, you may have chosen the wrong list type; that is a finding about your own direction and it counts.
4. Step 4, break it on purpose. Ask Claude to make a copy of the page called broken.html and, in the copy only, replace the main element and both section elements with plain divs, changing nothing else, saved in the same folder. Open broken.html and repeat Steps 1 and 2. What you will find: the headings list still works because headings are their own elements, but the region walk loses "main" entirely. The words and the visual look are the same, yet part of the page's meaning is gone. Write three or four sentences on what disappeared from the audit and what that tells you about why semantic elements exist, even though a visual check would never notice a difference. Cleanup: ask Claude to delete broken.html or move it out of the folder, and do not commit it.

Key terms:
Semantic element — an element whose name describes its meaning or role, such as header, nav, main, section, footer.
div — a generic container with no meaning; it does not announce as a region.
Region walk — jumping between landmarks: banner, navigation, main, content information.
Ordered list (ol) — a numbered list for content whose order matters.
Unordered list (ul) — a bulleted list for content whose order does not matter.
Headings outline — the structure heard in the headings list.

What you are asked to do:
- Run Steps 1 to 3, write what you heard under each, and direct fixes for any failures.
- Do the Step 4 experiment on broken.html, write three or four sentences of findings, then delete or move broken.html without committing it.
- Verdict: write the two-or-three-sentence verdict at the end of the lab (its prompts are in the original file), quoting the exact fix request you gave Claude word for word; the precision of that request is part of the grade.

Watch out for:
- A missing region usually means a div was used where a semantic element belongs.
- Choosing ordered versus unordered lists should be based on whether order matters.
- Do not commit broken.html.
