Summary of: ai04_AudioLab.md
Course: Web Design AI track (archived version)
Lesson: ai04 Images Media and Tables
Type of document: audio lab (screen reader audit of your own build, "Media Under Audit")

In one sentence:
This lab audits your media page by ear: the alt text on every image, a silent decorative image, a correct data table compared with a deliberately lazy one, the audio and video controls, and whether alt text and captions repeat each other.

Main ideas:
Your media page has four content images with alt text you wrote yourself, one decorative image that should be silent, audio and video players, and a data table. Keep the brief nearby, describe failures to Claude precisely, and verify the fixes.

1. Step 1, the graphics pass. Alt text is what a screen reader speaks in place of an image. You wrote your alt texts and dictated them to Claude, so this checks they made it into the code word for word. It also checks that the decorative image announces nothing, because empty alt tells screen readers to skip it as decoration. Use the next-graphic command through the whole page. You should hear your four alt texts exactly as written, with only four stops; the decorative image should not be a stop. Two different failures:
- If the audit stops on the decorative image and says "graphic" or reads a description, its alt is not empty. Tell Claude the decorative divider is announcing and to give it an empty alt attribute (alt equals two quotation marks with nothing between) and save.
- If an image announces a filename, like "IMG underscore 4 4 7 2 dot jpg," its alt is missing entirely. Tell Claude that image has no alt attribute and to add the alt text you wrote, then save.
2. Step 2, the correct table cell by cell. Your table should announce headers as you move through the data, like the ai00 practice page. That comes from real header cells with scope, plus a caption, both required by the brief. Jump to the table, listen for the caption, then walk down one data column. You should hear the caption on arrival and then the column header with each value, for example "Points, 24," "Points, 31," so every number carries its meaning. If you get bare values, the header cells are probably ordinary cells; ask Claude to make the first row real header cells with the scope attribute, add a caption if missing, and save.
3. Step 3, the lazy table, same walk. The brief had Claude build the same table badly in lazy-table.html: every cell ordinary, no caption, same data and same look. Open it and repeat Step 2. You should notice no caption and bare values with nothing telling you which column you are in. Try going to a cell in the middle and asking what the number means; in the correct table you would know, but here you would have to count columns and memorize the top row. Write two or three sentences describing the difference; the lab says you will reuse this comparison skill on the December exams and in html11's critique. Cleanup: have Claude delete lazy-table.html or move it out of the folder, and do not commit it.
4. Step 4, media players. The brief required the controls attribute, which gives each player real buttons such as play, pause, and volume. Tab into the video player, then the audio player, listening to what each control announces, and try play and pause on each. You should hear named controls such as "play button," "mute button," and a volume slider, reachable one by one, with play and pause working. If Tab skips a player or controls do not activate, the controls attribute is likely missing; ask Claude to check the element has it, fix it, and save.
5. Step 5, alt versus caption. Two images are inside figure elements with visible captions. A screen reader announces both the alt text and the caption, so if they say the same thing the visitor hears it twice and one is wasted. Alt describes what is in the image; the caption adds context around it. Read continuously through both figures. You should hear two different, complementary pieces of information; the lab's example is alt "Three loaves of sourdough cooling on a wire rack" with caption "Saturday's first batch, gone by nine a.m." If they repeat, decide which to rewrite (usually the caption), and tell Claude to keep the alt and rewrite the caption to add context, giving your new caption, then save.

Key terms:
Alt text — the text a screen reader speaks in place of an image.
Empty alt (alt="") — marks an image as decorative so screen readers skip it.
Missing alt — no alt attribute at all; screen readers may read the filename instead.
Header cell with scope — a th cell whose scope attribute ties it to its column or row so it is announced with each value.
Caption (table) — the title of a table, announced on arrival.
controls attribute — gives audio and video players usable buttons.
figure and figcaption — a figure wrapper and its visible caption.

What you are asked to do:
- Steps 1, 2, 4, and 5: perform each check, write what you heard, and direct fixes.
- Step 3: compare the lazy table, write two or three sentences, then delete or move lazy-table.html without committing it.
- Verdict: write the three-sentence verdict at the end of the lab (its prompts are in the original file), including an exact fix request you gave Claude; it is part of the completed AudioLab you turn in.

Watch out for:
- Empty alt and missing alt are different problems with different fixes.
- Tables without real header cells and a caption read as bare numbers.
- Media without controls may be unreachable by keyboard.
- Alt text and captions should not repeat each other.
- Correction: put at least two of your four images inside figure elements with visible figcaptions, which satisfies both documents. The AudioLab's Step 5 expects two captioned figures, while the BuildBrief names figure and figcaption without giving a number.
