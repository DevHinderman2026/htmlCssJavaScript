In one sentence:
This guide covers segment 11d, where you rebuild your client's site with no code, first in Google Sites and then in WordPress.com, while taking notes on what these tools trade away for ease.

Main ideas:
1. Two builds in about a day and a half with zero code. The point is not that builders are easy; it is to understand exactly what they give up in exchange for being easy, which you can see only because you have already built a site the hard way.
2. Build 1, Google Sites, about 45 minutes and deliberately quick:
- Go to sites.google.com and make a new site for your client.
- Recreate your home page and one other page: header, nav, text, and images.
- Publish it and note the URL.
While working, keep a running list of what was faster than hand-coding and what you wanted to do but Sites would not allow. Try adding your dark mode, and try controlling exactly where an image sits.
3. Build 2, WordPress.com, a real CMS (competency 6.5.4). WordPress powers roughly 40 percent of the web. The free hosted tier at wordpress.com skips installation (the "hosted" part; recall the html10 slide on what installing involves) and puts you straight into configuring a CMS:
- Create a free site for your client.
- Pick a theme. Notice that the theme is a template system and your content pours into it.
- Build your pages, set up the menu (the navigation), and add a post, which is the announcement or news feature your client needs. Posts are what a static site cannot give a non-coder.
- Explore the dashboard: users and roles, themes, and the plugin directory (look, do not install). Connect what you see to the vocabulary from the html10 CMS slide.
- Publish and note the URL.
4. Comparison notes, which feed segment 11f. For each builder, record: build time; what the client's non-coder could maintain alone; what is impossible in the builder that your hand-coded site does; what it costs to remove ads or get a real domain; and, for WordPress, where the database and template structure showed itself.

Key terms:
Site builder — a no-code tool for making websites from ready-made blocks, like Google Sites.
CMS — content management system; software like WordPress that stores content in a database and displays it through templates so non-coders can update it.
Hosted — the service runs the software for you, so there is nothing to install.
Theme — a WordPress template system that controls the look; your content fills it.
Menu — WordPress's navigation setup.
Post — a dated news or announcement entry in WordPress.
Dashboard — the WordPress admin area.
Users and roles — accounts with different permission levels.
Plugin — an add-on that extends WordPress features.

What you are asked to do:
- Build a Google Sites version (home page plus one other page), publish it, and record the URL.
- Build a WordPress.com version with a theme, pages, a menu, and at least one post; explore the dashboard without installing plugins; publish and record the URL.
- Record the comparison notes for each builder listed above.
- Commit html11_BuilderNotes_lastname.md (with your last name) containing both URLs and your observations. The sites themselves live on Google and WordPress; the URLs are the artifact you turn in.

Watch out for:
- Do not install plugins; only look at the plugin directory.
- Actively test the builders' limits (dark mode, exact image placement) so you have evidence for the comparison.
- The Unit Guide notes that these sites still need a check against the district web filter before this segment starts.
