Course: HTML CSS JavaScript track
Lesson: html11 Build It Three Ways
Type of document: template (segment 11g, handoff and presentation)

In one sentence:
This template describes the final handoff document you write for your client and the structure of your 5-minute presentation.

Main ideas:
1. Real projects end with a handoff, not a shrug. You write as if the client were real people about to take the keys to their site.
2. The handoff has four parts: what you are delivering, how to update it, a support plan, and a rollout note.
3. The presentation is a timed 5-minute talk given live in class.

Key terms:
Handoff — the document that transfers a finished project to the client with everything they need to run it.
Support plan — what tends to break, how to fix it, and where accounts and files are kept.
Rollout — how the site is launched and announced, including the final check before it goes public.

What you are asked to do:
Write html11_Handoff_lastname.md (with your last name) for your client, with these four sections:
1. What you are delivering: the recommended build (from your 11f verdict), its URL or location, and what the other two builds taught you that shaped the recommendation.
2. How to update it: three common updates (change the hours, add a photo, post an announcement), each written as numbered steps a non-coder can follow.
3. Support plan: what breaks most often and what to do about it; where the files and accounts live; and what you would build next with another month.
4. Rollout note: how you would announce or launch the site, including when it goes live, who checks it first, and the final review step before the URL goes public.

Give a 5-minute presentation with these timed parts:
1. The client and what they needed (30 seconds).
2. All three builds, shown on screen (2 minutes).
3. The most surprising row of your matrix (1 minute).
4. Your verdict and why (1 minute).
5. One thing you would tell your past self from last August about web development (30 seconds).

Commit the handoff document. Presentations are given live, so nothing is committed for them.

Watch out for:
- Update steps must be simple enough for a non-coder; number every step.
- Your recommendation must match the verdict from your 11f comparison.

Connects to:
Builds on the Compare Matrix verdict (11f); covers the rollout and handoff competencies named in the Unit Guide.
