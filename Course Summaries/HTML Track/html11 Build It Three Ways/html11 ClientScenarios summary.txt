Lesson: html11 Build It Three Ways
Type of document: client scenarios (project brief options)

In one sentence:
This document lists the five client scenarios you choose from; the client you pick stays yours for all three builds.

Main ideas:
1. Every scenario has the same shape: a small organization, real content needs, and a reason they cannot decide how to build their site. Your comparison of the three builds answers that question for them.
2. Scenario 1, Riverbend Youth Soccer League. Serves 300 families. Needs schedules (a table), registration information with a contact form, field locations, a photo gallery, and weather-cancellation announcements. The league secretary, who is not a coder, must be able to update announcements.
3. Scenario 2, The Rusty Spoon Diner. A 40-year-old family restaurant. Needs a menu with prices, hours and location, a history page with old photos, and a contact form for party bookings. The owner wants the site to "feel like us, not like a chain."
4. Scenario 3, Medina Makers Club. A community workshop with 3D printers, a laser cutter, and a woodshop. Needs an equipment list with safety information, a membership signup form, a class calendar, and a project gallery. Members keep asking for dark mode because they check the site from the shop floor.
5. Scenario 4, Paws and Whiskers Rescue. An animal shelter. Needs adoptable-pet profiles (a photo and details for each), an adoption application form, donation information, and a volunteer page. New pets arrive every week, so updates must be easy.
6. Scenario 5, your own, with teacher approval required. A real club, team, business, or organization you know. At minimum it must need a multi-page structure, a data table, a form, a gallery, and regular content updates by a non-coder.

Key terms:
Client — the organization you are building the website for.
Non-coder maintainer — the person at the client (like the league secretary) who must update the site without writing code; this is a key factor in your final recommendation.

What you are asked to do:
- Pick exactly one scenario. It stays your client for all three builds.
- If you choose your own, get teacher approval, and make sure it meets all five minimum needs: multiple pages, a data table, a form, a gallery, and regular updates by a non-coder.

Watch out for:
- Pay attention to each client's hidden deciding factor (easy updates by a non-coder, a unique feel, dark mode, weekly new content). It should shape your verdict.

Connects to:
Your chosen client feeds the Proposal Template (11a) and every later segment of this unit.
