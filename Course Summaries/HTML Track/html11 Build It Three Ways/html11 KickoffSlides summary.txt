In one sentence:
These kickoff slides introduce the Build It Three Ways project: one client, three builds, and a professional verdict, plus the full deliverables list.

Main ideas:
1. The idea behind the project: now that you can hand-code a website, you should know whether professionals actually build sites that way. The answer is yes and no. Real developers choose between three approaches:
- Hand-coding: total control, but slowest.
- AI generation: fast, but someone has to catch its mistakes, and that someone is you.
- Builders and CMSes: fastest and least control, but the client can update the site themselves.
Professionals do not have a favorite approach; they have a recommendation for each client.
2. Your client. Pick one scenario (soccer league, diner, makers club, animal rescue) or pitch your own. That client stays yours for the whole block. The first deliverable is a one-page proposal covering scope, audience, objectives, timeline, and deliverables, because real projects start with planning on paper, not code.
3. The three builds:
- Hand-coded: your semester site, polished, with dark mode and mobile nav. About 1 to 2 days.
- AI-generated: uses your direction skills; you name the components and then critique the output. About 2 days.
- Builders: Google Sites, then WordPress, which is a real CMS. About 1 to 2 days.
Then you run the same usability checklist on all three, with no favoritism; your own build gets no free passes.
4. The verdict: a comparison matrix plus one paragraph answering which build your client should use. There is no single right answer, only a well-argued one.
5. Why the AI critique is graded hardest: anyone can generate a website, but professionals can say what is wrong with it. Classic problems are div soup (divs everywhere instead of semantic elements), missing alt text, fake headings, and unlabeled inputs. Spotting errors is also exactly what the December exams ask, so this is exam prep in a project costume.

Key terms:
Scope — what the project will and will not include.
Div soup — markup made almost entirely of div elements where semantic elements like header, nav, main, or section belong.
Fake heading — text styled to look like a heading instead of using a real heading tag like h2.
Usability checklist — the same set of tests applied to every build.
Verdict — your argued recommendation of which build the client should use.

What you are asked to do:
Deliverables checklist:
- 11a: proposal.
- 11b: polished hand-coded site with its features working.
- 11c: AI site, prompt log, and critique with 5 or more findings.
- 11d: builder notes and two live URLs.
- 11e: optimized images and an SVG logo.
- 11f: checklist results, matrix, and verdict.
- 11g: handoff document and 5-minute presentation.

Watch out for:
- Be equally strict with your own hand-coded build.
- The AI critique carries the most weight, so practice spotting div soup, missing alt text, fake headings, and unlabeled inputs; these also appear on the exams.
