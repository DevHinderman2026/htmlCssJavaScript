In one sentence:
This worksheet teaches a study method where, for every practice question, you explain why each wrong answer is wrong, and asks you to apply it to the 12 mock quiz questions in the Certiport study guide.

Main ideas:
It is part of the opt-in Certiport lane, day 3, and works with the mock quiz in Module 13 of the Certiport HTML and CSS study guide.

The method: answering a question tests you once, but explaining why each wrong choice is wrong tests you three times. For each mock quiz question:
1. Answer it without notes.
2. Check your answer against the study guide.
3. For every wrong option, write one sentence explaining why a test writer would include it and why it is wrong.
4. Tag the question as knew it, guessed it, or missed it. A guess counts as a miss, so re-study those modules.

Worked example. Question: which CSS selector has the highest specificity: a) p, b) .intro, c) #main, d) p.intro?
- The answer is c, the ID, because an ID outranks any combination of classes and elements.
- a is wrong because a bare element selector is the weakest; it is there to catch people who think the first option is strongest.
- b is wrong because a class beats an element but loses to an ID; it catches people who only memorized half the ladder.
- d is wrong because an element plus a class scores (0,1,1), which still loses to an ID at (1,0,0). The trap is that d looks stronger because it is longer. Length is not specificity.
The guide points out that last insight is the kind of thing only this method produces.

Key terms:
distractor — a wrong answer choice designed to look right.
autopsy — a careful after-the-fact examination, here of why answers are wrong.
knew, guessed, missed — the three tags for sorting your results; guessed counts as missed.

What you are asked to do:
For each of the study guide's 12 mock questions, fill in a block with: the question number, your answer, the correct answer, and your tag (knew, guessed or missed); then one line for each of the three wrong options explaining why it is tempting and why it is wrong. Commit a file named html12_Autopsy_lastname.md containing all 12 blocks plus a final line naming the two modules you will re-read tonight, based on your guessed and missed tags.

Watch out for:
- Being honest about guesses matters; treating a lucky guess as "knew it" hides a weak spot.
