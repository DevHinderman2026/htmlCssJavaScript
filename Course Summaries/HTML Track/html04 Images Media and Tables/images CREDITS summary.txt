In one sentence:
This file credits the photographers and licenses for the practice photos in the lesson's images folder and serves as the model for how to credit photos on your own site.

Main ideas:
1. The photos come from Wikimedia Commons and are free to use under Creative Commons licenses. When you use a Creative Commons photo on your own site, credit the photographer in this same way: file, photo title, photographer, and license.

Key terms:
Creative Commons (CC) — a family of licenses that let creators allow free reuse under stated conditions.
CC BY — you may reuse the work if you credit the creator.
CC BY-SA — you must credit the creator and share any adapted version under the same license (SA means share-alike).
Wikimedia Commons — a free media library where these photos came from.

What you are asked to do:
Nothing directly. The html04 Project tells you to use this format when crediting any photo on your gallery page that you did not take.

Watch out for:
- Credit every photo you did not take, either in its caption or in a credits line at the bottom of the page.
- Heads up: the images folder also holds five small PNG files (product-name.png and four smartwatch images: detail, front view, lifestyle, and side view) that no lesson document uses and this file does not credit. You can ignore them; if you do use one on a page, its source is unknown, so ask Mr. McMaster before publishing it.
