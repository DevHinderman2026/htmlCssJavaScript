In one sentence:
This is the one graded item for Lesson 04: add a photo gallery page, a 20-second Lego stop-motion promo video you make yourself, and a comparison table to your 3-page mini-site from the Lesson 03 DIY task, then validate and push.

Main ideas:
1. What gets added. The a, b, and c tasks are practice; this project is graded. You add three things: a photo gallery on a new page, gallery.html; a 20-second promo video on your Home page; and a comparison table on your About page.
2. No CSS. Do not add style attributes or style blocks anywhere. In Lesson 05 you will write one stylesheet that styles every page of this site, so the HTML must stay clean.
3. Order and timing. Start the video early, because recording slots and shooting take time. Do the gallery and table whenever the matching a, b, or c task is done. Everything must be pushed by Friday.
4. Folder layout and page changes. Work in your Lesson 03 site folder. index.html gets Gallery in both navs, a promo section with the video, and a link jumping to it. about.html gets Gallery in both navs and a compare section with the table. contact.html only gets Gallery in both navs. The new gallery.html shares the same header, nav, and footer and holds the photos. New folders: images (your 3 or more photos) and media (promo-lastname.mp4).
6. Part 1a, making the gallery page. Copy index.html and rename the copy gallery.html so the header, nav, and footer come along with no retyping. Change its title to "Your Site Name - Gallery" and delete everything inside main. Add a Gallery link to the header nav and the footer nav on all four pages, which is 8 navs in total. Every nav should list, in order, Home (index.html), About (about.html), Gallery (gallery.html), Contact (contact.html). Then open each page and click every nav link.
7. Part 1b, adding photos. Make a folder named images next to your HTML files and put at least 3 topic-related photos in it, with short names and no spaces, such as latte.jpg or band-stage.jpg. Inside main on gallery.html, add a section with id="photos" and an h2 "Photo Gallery", then one figure per photo.
Code: <img src="images/latte.jpg" alt="Latte with a leaf drawn in the foam" width="400" height="300">
This is the example image inside a figure: a short descriptive alt, plus width and height. It is followed by a figcaption reading "Our house latte".
Every photo needs alt text, width and height, a figure around it, and a figcaption. Photos you did not take need a credit, either in the caption or in a credits line at the bottom of the page. Free sources: Wikimedia Commons, Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay. Download the file rather than linking to the site. The credit format is shown in images/CREDITS.md.
8. Part 2, the promo video overview. A 20-second ad for your site acted out by Lego figures; you are not on camera. Work with a partner to time scripts, run the mic, move figures, and figure out Audacity and Canva, but each person makes their own video for their own site. Hard limits: 20 seconds maximum, under 10 MB, Canva's built-in music only, and school-appropriate (Mr. McMaster and your parents could watch it).
9. Part 2a, script on paper. Seconds 0 to 4: the problem or hook. Seconds 4 to 14: the site and 2 or 3 things on it. Seconds 14 to 20: closing line with the site name. Aim for about 45 words, two characters at most, and say the site name at the end. Read it aloud while your partner times you; if it runs over 17 seconds, cut words. Write a shot list, one line per picture. Show Mr. McMaster the timed script before you record.
10. Part 2b, voice in Audacity on the Macs. Partners take turns recording and running Audacity; shoot pictures while waiting. Keep the mic about ten inches away and speak clearly at normal volume. Record a test line: the waveform should reach about half to two-thirds of the track height (move closer if small, back off if it hits the top). Record the script; after a mistake, pause, repeat the line, and continue. Select and Delete bad parts, join what remains, and trim dead air at both ends. Optional: Select All, then Effect, Volume and Compression, Normalize, Apply. Use File, Export Audio to save an MP3 named promo-voice-lastname (or similar) in Documents, and also Save Project there. Put the MP3 in your Google Drive to reach it from Canva.
11. Part 2c, pictures on a phone. Stop motion is many stills played fast: 2 per second (about 40 pictures) or 3 per second (about 60, smoother). Use a small desk set, plain background, one or two props. The phone must not move: prop it on books, tape it, or rig a stand. Steady light, no flash, landscape for every shot. Move figures a tiny bit each time (big moves look like teleporting) and take extras of the final pose for the site name. Transfer via the Canva phone app, email, or Google Drive.
12. Part 2d, Canva (report to Mr. M any steps that seem off). Create a Video design (landscape, 1920 by 1080). Upload the MP3 and pictures, and drag the MP3 onto the timeline first since it sets the length. Add one page per picture stretched to fill, then set all pages to 0.5 seconds (2 per second) or 0.3 seconds (3 per second). Rearrange so pictures match the lines. Put the site name as big text in one font on the last pages. Add music from Elements, Audio at about 15 to 20 percent volume. Total length must be 20.0 seconds or less; if over, cut pictures, not words. Optional, and preferred by Mr. McMaster when time allows: change the voice speed (too fast if your partner cannot repeat the site name) and add quiet sound effects like a door or ding.
13. Part 2e, export and place it. Share, Download, MP4 at 720p (not 1080p). Rename it promo-lastname.mp4 (real last name, lowercase, no spaces). It must be under 10 MB; re-export at lower quality if not. Put it in the media folder. In index.html, inside main, right after your first section, add a section with id="promo", an h2 "Watch the Promo", and a video element with width 640, height 360, and controls, holding a source of media/promo-lastname.mp4 with type video/mp4 and the fallback sentence.
14. Page anchor. Near the top of main on index.html, add a paragraph link that jumps to the video, the way you did in Lesson 03.
Code: <p><a href="#promo">Watch our 20-second promo</a></p>
The href value #promo points to the element whose id is promo, so clicking it jumps down to the video section.
If the video is not finished, add the section now with the planned file name; it will work once the file is in the media folder.
15. Part 3, the comparison table on about.html. Compare 3 or more things from your topic. Ideas: coffee shop drinks with small, medium, and large prices; band tour dates with city, venue, date, ticket price; pet care services with dog, cat, and small pet prices; fitness classes with day, time, length, level; tech blog tools or projects with what it is, language, and year. The coffee example has a section with id="compare", an h2 "Our Drinks", a caption "Drink Prices by Size", a header row of Drink, Small, Medium, Large, a Latte row priced $3.50, $4.25, $5.00, and a final row with one td using colspan="4" saying prices include tax and oat milk is 50 cents extra. Use your own topic and data; do not copy the coffee example unless your site really is a coffee shop.
16. Borders. Your table will have no lines around cells, which is expected. The 04b and 04c tasks had a style block drawing borders; leave it out here. You will add borders yourself in Lesson 05.
17. Part 4, finish and push. Open all 4 pages and click every header and footer nav link. On index.html, click the promo link and play the video with sound. On gallery.html, check every photo appears. Run each page through validator.w3.org and fix errors. Commit and push, then confirm on github.com that gallery.html, the images folder, and the media folder all arrived.

Key terms:
page anchor — a link whose href is a hash plus an id, jumping to that part of the page.
stop motion — animation made from many still photos played quickly.
Audacity — audio recording and editing program used for the voice track.
Normalize — Audacity effect that evens out the volume.
Canva — online design tool used to assemble the video.
720p — the export resolution required, which keeps the file small.
W3C validator — online HTML error checker.

Due: everything pushed by Friday.

Watch out for:
- Image or video will not show: it is the path. The src must match the file name exactly, including capital letters and extension, and the file must be in the right folder.
- Gallery link works on some pages but not others: you missed one of the 8 navs.
- Audacity shows a flat line: wrong input is selected; check the mic dropdown and System Settings, Privacy and Security, Microphone.
- Voice sounds far away: get closer. Pops on P and B sounds: talk across the mic, not into it.
- Jumpy stop motion: moves were too big or the camera moved.
- Sideways pictures in Canva: they were shot in portrait; rotate or reshoot.
- Video over 20 seconds: delete pictures, not voice.
- MP4 over 10 MB: export at 720p; if still too big, it is probably over 20 seconds.
- No sound on the site: the voice volume is 0 in Canva or the computer is muted.
- Table has no lines: normal without CSS; borders come in Lesson 05.
- Confused by colspan: sketch the table and count columns in each row; every row must add up to the same total.
