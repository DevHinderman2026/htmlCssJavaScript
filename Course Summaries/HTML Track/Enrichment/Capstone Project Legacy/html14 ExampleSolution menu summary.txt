In one sentence:
This is the Menu page of the sample Mountain View Coffee Co. site, and it demonstrates the capstone's required data table along with menu items built from repeated article elements.

Main ideas:
1. Head: UTF-8 charset, viewport meta tag, the title "Menu (separator bar) Mountain View Coffee Co.," a meta description about specialty coffee drinks, pastries, and light food, an og:title tag, and the shared stylesheet link.
2. Header and navigation match all other pages, with the Menu link marked active.
3. The main landmark begins with a page header section: h1 "Our Menu" and the tagline "Crafted with care, served with passion."
4. Espresso Drinks section (h2) with a short description paragraph and six menu items. Each menu item is an article containing an h3 name, a description paragraph, and a price paragraph. Items: Espresso Shot (single 2.50, double 3.50), Americano (3.50 to 4.50), Latte (4.50 to 5.50), Cappuccino (4.50 to 5.50), Macchiato (3.75 to 4.75), Mocha (5.00 to 6.00). Prices are in dollars.
5. Coffee Comparison Guide section (h2), the required table. Comments in the code call it out as "the TABLE required by the capstone project." Its structure:
- A table element with class comparison-table.
- A thead with one row of six th header cells: Drink Name, Espresso, Milk, Foam, Strength, Best For.
- A tbody with six rows, one per drink. The first cell of each row holds the drink name in bold.
- Row data: Espresso has 2 ounces of espresso, no milk, no foam, strength 5 of 5, best for those who want pure coffee. Americano: 1 to 2 ounces, no milk, no foam, 4 of 5, drip coffee lovers. Latte: 1 to 2 ounces, 8 to 10 ounces milk, small foam, 2 of 5, milk lovers. Cappuccino: 1 to 2 ounces, 3 to 4 ounces milk, large foam, 3 of 5, balance of coffee and milk. Macchiato: 1 to 2 ounces, a quarter ounce milk, small foam, 4 of 5, coffee-forward with a hint of milk. Mocha: 1 to 2 ounces, 8 to 10 ounces milk, small foam, 3 of 5, chocolate and coffee lovers.
- Strength is shown with filled and empty star characters rather than numbers.
6. Cold Drinks section (h2) with four items: Iced Coffee, Cold Brew (steeped 12 or more hours), Iced Latte, Iced Mocha, priced from 3.50 to 6.00.
7. Fresh Pastries and Food section (h2), noting everything is baked daily by pastry chef Alex, with six items: Croissant, Blueberry Muffin, Almond Scone, Chocolate Chip Cookie, Sandwich (Italian or Chicken), and Seasonal Salad, priced from 3.50 to 10.99.
8. Specialty Drinks section (h2) with four rotating items: Seasonal Latte (currently vanilla and cinnamon), Affogato (ice cream with a hot espresso shot), Cortado (1 to 1 espresso and milk), and Pour Over.

10. This page has no images at all; it relies on text, cards, and the table.

Key terms:
Table (written <table>) — an element for data arranged in rows and columns.
thead — the table section holding the header row.
tbody — the table section holding the data rows.
tr — a table row.
th — a header cell that labels a column.
td — a regular data cell.
Article (written <article>) — used here for each self-contained menu item.
Class comparison-table — the hook the stylesheet uses to make this table scroll sideways on phones.

What you are asked to do:
Nothing directly; this is a model for the capstone's required table. The rubric's Excellent level wants 4 or more columns, 5 or more rows, clear headers, and visual styling; this table has 6 columns and 6 data rows.

Watch out for:
- Using thead, tbody, and th makes the table easier for screen readers, because each data cell can be announced with its column header.
- The table has no caption element and no scope attributes. It relies on the h2 above it as its title. Adding a caption (written <caption>) is a simple accessibility improvement for your own tables.
- The Strength column uses star symbols. A screen reader may read each one as "black star" or "white star," which is tedious; a number such as "4 out of 5" is clearer.
- Correction: use one h1 per page, as the rubric and common best practice require. Like the About and Contact pages, this page has two h1 elements (site name and "Our Menu"); in your own pages, keep the page title as the only h1.
- The capstone spec names this page services.html; the example uses a menu page instead, which is fine for a cafe theme.
