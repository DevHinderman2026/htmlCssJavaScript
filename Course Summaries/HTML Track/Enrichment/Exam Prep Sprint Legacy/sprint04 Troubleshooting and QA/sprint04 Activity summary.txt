Summary of: sprint04_Activity.md
Course: HTML CSS JavaScript track
Lesson: sprint04 Troubleshooting and QA
Type of document: activity worksheet

In one sentence:
A 45-minute fill-in worksheet with three scenarios where you troubleshoot a missing navbar, write five QA test cases for a pizza order form, and plan a small client website project, followed by three reflection questions.

Main ideas:
1. The worksheet puts the three parts of sprint04 into practice: troubleshooting with the Spot the Differences method, writing formal test cases, and project planning.
2. Each part gives a realistic scenario and blank lines to fill in. There is a space for your name and date at the top.
3. It ends with a self-check list and the instruction to turn it in when complete.

Key terms:
Spot the Differences — comparing a working page to a broken one to find what differs.
root cause — the real underlying reason for a problem.
test case — Test ID, Description, Steps, Expected Result, Actual Result and Pass or Fail.
scope — what the project includes and excludes.
stakeholders — people invested in the project.
target audience — who will use the site.
milestone — a dated checkpoint with a deliverable.
critical path — tasks that cannot be delayed without delaying the whole project.
acceptance criteria — conditions the client needs met to sign off.
risk assessment — listing what could go wrong and how to prevent it.

What you are asked to do:
Scenario 1, troubleshooting with Spot the Differences. A coffee shop site's navbar shows on the Homepage but is missing entirely on the About page. The worksheet says both pages have the correct CSS file linked in the head and the navbar HTML is identical. Compare the working Homepage to the broken About page and write out all five Spot the Differences steps on the worksheet, from your first comparison through verifying the fix, including a list of 3 specific things you would check, such as file paths, CSS selectors or JavaScript.

Scenario 2, writing QA test cases. A pizzeria's Pizza Ordering Form lets customers enter their name, pick a size (Small, Medium, Large), choose toppings with checkboxes, enter a delivery address, and click "Place Order". Write 5 test cases, each with Test ID, Description, numbered Steps, Expected Result, Actual Result (filled in during testing) and Pass or Fail:
1. TC-001, Valid Order Submission (5 step lines).
2. TC-002, Missing Required Field (Name) (4 step lines).
3. TC-003, Topping Selection and Display (5 step lines).
4. TC-004, Invalid Email Address (4 step lines).
5. TC-005, Delivery Address Validation (5 step lines).

Scenario 3, project planning worksheet. Client: a dog grooming business called "Pawsome Grooming". Requirements: a 4-page site (Home, Services, Gallery, Contact); budget 500 dollars; deadline 3 weeks (21 days) from today; staff must be scheduled during the timeline; the client has limited tech knowledge. Complete:
1. Project scope: what IS included (pages, features) and what is NOT included for this budget and timeline, about 4 lines each.
2. Stakeholders: list 4. Target audience: describe who will use the site, with demographics and needs.
3. Milestones: write today's date, then 3 major milestones, each with a name, a date (and how many days from today), and what is delivered. Be realistic for 3 weeks.
4. Critical path: list 5 tasks that cannot be delayed without missing the deadline.
5. Acceptance criteria: list 6 conditions that must be true for the client to sign off.
6. Risk assessment: list 3 risks of the tight timeline and one prevention for each.

Answer the three reflection questions at the end of the original worksheet in complete sentences (the self-check requires them before you turn it in).

Self-check before turning in: Scenario 1 steps written clearly; all 5 test cases completed with specific steps; scope, stakeholders, milestones, critical path and acceptance criteria all filled in; reflection questions answered in complete sentences. Then turn it in. No point values are given.

Watch out for:
- A request like a shopping cart on Day 10 is scope creep; the scope and design brief are your basis for the answer.
- A delay on a critical path task, like late design approval, pushes the whole deadline back unless something else changes.
- Heads up: Scenario 1 says both pages link the CSS file correctly, yet a later step imagines a typo in the About page's stylesheet path (css/styles.css versus css/style.css). The two cannot both be true, so treat that step as a what-if and answer it as written.
- Heads up: TC-004 asks you to test an invalid email address, but the pizza form as described has no email field. Assume the form also has an email field (for example for an order confirmation) and say so in that test case's description.
